## Introduction: Why AI Transformation Is Like Fitness

AI today is like a gym membership—everyone gets one, then posts about it.

Setting up AI labs, purchasing the most advanced models, announcing partnerships with tech giants... These actions sound futuristic. But as Alexander Sukharevsky, head of McKinsey Quantum Black, put it bluntly in an in-depth interview: Getting the membership is just the beginning. The real challenge is whether you're willing to "sweat" on the treadmill every day.

The problem is, most companies are satisfied with the "membership" action but hesitate to "sweat it out" on real business transformation. They think purchasing the most advanced technology automatically delivers business value. The result? A few beautiful PowerPoint decks, scattered proof-of-concept projects, and no movement in core business metrics.

**Getting a membership ≠ Working out. Technology procurement ≠ Value unlock.**

AI transformation is never just a technology problem—it's a profound organizational reshaping. It's not a multiple-choice question; it's a "complete recipe" that must be answered correctly in every part.

Sukharevsky has a more vivid metaphor for Agents: Think of an Agent as your "junior colleague"—extremely hardworking, super-fast learning, 24/7 availability, always on call. But remember, **you're the boss, and you're responsible when things go wrong**. No matter how smart or hardworking this colleague is, the ultimate responsibility always rests with you. This isn't shifting blame—it's clarifying boundaries: Agents do the work; humans judge, correct, and take responsibility.

## I. The "Six-Element Framework" for Value Unlock: A Systematic Project

Sukharevsky's research yields a brutal conclusion: The success rate of AI transformation is extremely low because it's a systematic project. You can't optimize just one or two elements; all elements must be coordinated.

McKinsey Quantum Black found in extensive enterprise transformation research: There are no shortcuts. All six elements must be in place:

| Element | Core Meaning | Common Pitfall |
|---------|--------------|----------------|
| **Business Domain Reinvention** | Focus on ≤2 core domains, completely reinvent business processes | Trying to cover everything, layering AI onto existing processes |
| **Data Products** | Build reusable data assets | Waiting for perfect data or cobbling together temporary datasets |
| **Architecture Reorganization** | Let data flow freely, break down department walls | Treating it as purely technical, ignoring political dimensions |
| **Governance & Operations** | Establish new rules for Agents, traditional governance fails | Managing Agents the same way as people |
| **Economic Model** | Calculate the full cost: technology + talent + change management | Counting only technology investment, ignoring hidden costs |
| **Integration & Implementation** | Make all six elements work together | Optimizing isolated points, lacking global perspective |

Sukharevsky has a line worth putting on every CEO's office wall: **If you set the wrong goal, it will execute at extreme speed in the wrong direction.**

In the Agent era, this risk is amplified tenfold. Agents execute faster, make decisions more automatically, and have broader impact—once they go wrong, the damage is exponential. Sukharevsky is clear: Agents aren't additional tools, but "new members" of the organization—the future competitive advantage depends on whether people, agents, data, business models, and judgment can effectively coordinate.

## II. From Theory to Practice: When "Six Elements" Meet "Compliance Deep Water"

Placing McKinsey's "Six-Element Framework" in China's current policy and market environment reveals a deeper bottleneck.

In 2026, the Cyberspace Administration, National Development and Reform Commission, and Ministry of Industry and Information Technology jointly issued the "Implementation Opinion on Standardized Application and Innovative Development of Intelligent Agents," elevating "safe, reliable, and trustworthy" to unprecedented strategic importance. Enterprise AI transformation has moved from early "technology exploration" to a deep-water zone where "compliance governance" and "scaled application" are equally important. Enterprises need not just a "gym" to build AI capabilities, but an "intelligent cockpit" where AI can run safely, compliantly, and collaboratively.

**What does the Implementation Opinion require?**

- **Safety baseline**: Data security compliance, sensitive data masking
- **Reliable operation**: Agent behavior traceable and auditable
- **Trustworthy governance**: Clear human-agent collaboration responsibility boundaries

Without these, it's like a self-driving car without brakes—no matter how fast it goes, you wouldn't dare ride it.

Governance isn't retrofitted—it must be installed from day one. Sukharevsky is clear: If governance isn't in place from the start, waiting until AI scales up to remember installing brakes doesn't just create chaos—it exposes you and your stakeholders to all kinds of risks. Conversely, companies with governance in place, because they've established trust, dare to let go.

**Governance isn't a shackle—it's an accelerator.**

## III. "Four Dilemmas" of Enterprise AI Transformation: The Gap Between Theory and Reality

Extensive enterprise AI implementation shows that theory is beautiful, reality is painful. Four pitfalls are most common.

### Dilemma 1: Coordination Becomes Silos

Sales Agents promise impossible delivery dates to hit targets. Supply Chain Agents see insufficient inventory and reject. Finance Agents calculate credit limits. Three Agents work separately, ignoring each other, creating new "AI silos."

Cross-domain business fragmentation means AI decisions only achieve local optimization, unable to support enterprise-wide coordination. McKinsey's "let data flow freely" is fundamentally about breaking department walls—**this is politics, not technology**.

### Dilemma 2: Governance Is a Black Box

AI decision processes are like black boxes—when something goes wrong, who's responsible? Nobody knows. No permission controls, no circuit breakers, no audit trails. Companies in complex business scenarios are constantly exposed to uncontrollable compliance risks and production hazards.

Managing Agents the way you manage people doesn't work. Traditional governance models fail in the Agent era.

### Dilemma 3: Degradation Upon Launch

Agents are smartest on launch day. Three months later? Business rules changed, models didn't. Six months later? Abandoned by business users.

AI capabilities gradually lag behind over time, becoming decorative. In Sukharevsky's words, Agents aren't tools—they're colleagues. They need continuous "feeding" and "training." Without feedback loops, Agents can't evolve.

### Dilemma 4: Unclear Costs

Every new scenario is developed from scratch, reinventing wheels. Development resources pour in, marginal costs don't come down, ROI can't be calculated.

Without a platform foundation, it gets heavier over time. McKinsey's "economic model" element emphasizes: Companies must calculate the full cost of transformation—not just technology investment, but also employee upskilling, change management, and all other costs.
